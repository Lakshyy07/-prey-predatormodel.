UNDERSTANDING OUR
MATHEMATICAL MODEL

A compact guide to the ecological ideas and numerical methods behind this simulation.

01

Predator–prey relationships

Prey grow when resources are available. Predators gain energy by consuming prey, and the interaction term saturates as prey become abundant.

02

Pollution & habitat

Pollution is added from an external source and prey activity, then removed naturally and through a saturating cleanup process. More pollution lowers the prey’s effective carrying capacity.

03

Carrying capacity

K(P) = K₀e−αP describes the pollution-adjusted limit on the prey population. The blue chart compares it with the clean-environment baseline.

04

Differential equations

Each equation gives an instantaneous rate of change. Together they describe how prey, predators, and pollution influence one another through time.

05

Population dynamics

Feedback between food supply and predator pressure can create rises and falls. Strong pressure or toxic conditions may drive a population toward extinction.

06

RK4 & reading the graphs

Fourth-order Runge–Kutta samples the coupled rates four times per step for accurate numerical integration. The time-series charts show change; the phase portrait shows prey–predator pairings.
